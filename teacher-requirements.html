// 01 · Overview

The CBC Teacher — A New Professional Standard

Kenya's CBC demands a fundamentally different kind of educator. The shift from 8-4-4's content-transmission model to CBC's competency facilitation model means teachers must be trained professionals who assess continuously, personalise learning, integrate technology, and connect curriculum to real-world contexts.

2025–2026 Senior Secondary Transition Notice

The first cohort of CBC learners enters Senior Secondary School (Grade 10) in 2025. This makes pathway-specific teacher deployment a matter of immediate national urgency. Schools, principals, and sub-county education officers must ensure all Grade 10–12 pathways are staffed by appropriately qualified and retooled teachers before the start of the 2025/2026 academic year. TSC circulars on senior secondary deployment should be consulted for the latest placement guidelines.

TSC

Teachers Service Commission (TSC)

The TSC is Kenya's constitutional employer of all public school teachers under Article 237 of the Constitution of Kenya (2010). It registers, deploys, promotes, disciplines, and terminates teachers. Under CBC, TSC has strengthened its requirements to include pathway-specific competency verification and mandatory retooling confirmation before senior secondary deployment.

1
Academic qualification — minimum degree in education or subject discipline plus teaching qualification
2
TSC registration — online application with academic documents, Certificate of Good Conduct, and prescribed fees
3
TSC induction — mandatory training covering CBC pedagogy, Teachers Code of Regulations, and child safeguarding
4
Annual licence renewal — via TSC portal; requires updated CPD records and TPAD compliance
Visit TSC portal →
KICD

Kenya Institute of Curriculum Development (KICD)

KICD is the statutory body responsible for developing, reviewing, and approving Kenya's school curricula under the Kenya Institute of Curriculum Development Act. Under CBC, KICD produces curriculum designs, assessment frameworks, and teacher competency standards — and runs the retooling programme that prepares teachers to deliver each pathway's subjects.

1
Curriculum designs — published per subject per level, specifying strands, sub-strands, learning outcomes, and assessment criteria
2
CBC retooling programme — structured face-to-face and online modules equipping teachers with CBC pedagogy and assessment skills
3
Teacher competency framework — defines professional knowledge, skills, and dispositions required for CBC delivery by pathway
4
Assessment tool training — trains teachers on all 13 prescribed tools including rubric design and portfolio management

Senior School Pathway

🔬 STEM Pathway Teachers

STEM teachers must combine deep content mastery with inquiry-based pedagogy, laboratory safety management, and the ability to connect abstract knowledge to real-world applications through project-based learning. From 2025, they serve Grade 10–12 learners on the most technically demanding pathway.

🔬
ℹ️

STEM Pathway Core Subjects (Grades 10–12)

All STEM learners study Mathematics, English, and one Kenyan language as compulsory core subjects. They then select from STEM electives: Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Computer Science, Agriculture, and Technical & Applied Sciences. Teachers must be qualified in the specific elective(s) they deliver.

Ability to design project-based assessments connecting mathematics to STEM applications: financial modelling, engineering calculations, and data analysis.
Skilled in differentiated instruction — providing tiered tasks (foundational, core, extension) to meet diverse learner needs within one class.
Knowledge of CBC assessment tools: analytic rubrics for mathematical investigations and portfolio-based competency recording formats.
CBC Specific: Mathematics teachers at senior level are expected to deliver contextualised learning — connecting every strand (numbers, algebra, geometry, statistics) to pathway-relevant applications such as engineering design problems, scientific data interpretation, and computer-aided calculation.

📌 Core CBC Pedagogical Competencies

Six competencies are non-negotiable for every CBC teacher, regardless of subject or pathway. They define the professional standard of CBC instruction and are assessed through TSC's Teacher Professional Appraisal and Development (TPAD) system and KICD quality assurance inspections.

📌

The 6 Pedagogical Competencies Every CBC Teacher Must Demonstrate

Unlike 8-4-4 where teacher quality was largely measured by exam pass rates, CBC measures teaching quality through demonstrated pedagogical practice. These six competencies are observable, assessable, and form the basis of annual professional development planning.

📊
Formative Assessment Design
Planning, implementing, and interpreting formative assessments using any of the 13 KICD-prescribed tools to continuously monitor and improve learner competency development.
e.g. Designing an analytic rubric for a Grade 10 Biology project; using an observation schedule during a Grade 8 group activity.
🎯
Differentiated Instruction
Adapting content, process, product, and the learning environment to meet the diverse needs, pace, interests, and readiness levels of individual learners within a single class.
e.g. Providing tiered tasks — foundational, core, and extension — within the same Mathematics lesson to support all ability levels.
🔨
Project-Based Learning (PBL)
Designing, scaffolding, and facilitating term-long or multi-week projects that require learners to investigate real problems, produce tangible outputs, and present findings to an audience.
e.g. A Grade 11 Geography project on urban heat islands in Nairobi, culminating in a report and community presentation.
💡
ICT Integration
Effective, purposeful use of digital tools — educational software, online resources, productivity suites, and communication platforms — to meaningfully enhance teaching and learning quality.
e.g. Using Google Classroom for assignments; PhET for Physics demonstrations; Khan Academy as a differentiation resource for diverse learners.
📁
Competency Recording & Reporting
Systematic tracking of individual learner progress across all seven CBC core competencies using KICD-approved recording formats, generating meaningful reports for parents and school management.
e.g. Correctly completing KICD Learner Progress Report Cards; maintaining a class competency profile register each assessment cycle.
🤝
Community Service Learning (CSL)
Planning and facilitating structured community engagement activities that connect curriculum learning outcomes to real community needs, building learners' citizenship competency and sense of social responsibility.
e.g. A Business Studies class running a financial literacy workshop for parents; a Geography class documenting environmental impact during a riverbank clean-up.
📋

Teacher Professional Appraisal & Development (TPAD) under CBC

All TSC-employed teachers undergo annual performance appraisal through the TPAD system. Under CBC, TPAD criteria have been updated to assess how effectively a teacher demonstrates the six pedagogical competencies, maintains assessment records, conducts CSL activities, and integrates ICT. TPAD scores influence promotion, deployment transfers, and CPD planning. Teachers undergo self-appraisal, peer observation, and principal appraisal each year. Poor TPAD performance can result in mandatory professional development placement.

SSS
10–12

Senior Secondary School (Grades 10–12) — Deployment Principles

The 2025/2026 academic year opens senior secondary implementation. TSC and the Ministry of Education have established these deployment principles:

1
Pathway matching — every deployed teacher must hold a qualification in the subject(s) they teach within their assigned pathway. Mismatched deployment is flagged during KICD quality assurance visits.
2
Retooling verification — schools and sub-county directors must verify that all Grade 10–12 teachers hold a valid KICD retooling certificate before the start of the academic year.
3
Communities of Practice (CoPs) — KICD encourages subject-based CoPs at school, zonal, and county level for peer learning and collaborative lesson planning.
4
Shortage mitigation — for niche subjects (Film & Animation, Sports Science), TSC may approve qualified industry practitioners on short-term contracts while degree-holding teachers are developed.
// TSC & KICD Registration Process

How to Qualify and Register as a CBC Teacher

This section covers the full journey — from completing academic qualifications to obtaining a TSC registration number, completing KICD retooling, and maintaining your professional licence. It applies to new graduates, career-changers, and in-service teachers transitioning to senior secondary CBC delivery.

01

Complete Accredited Academic Qualifications

All prospective teachers must complete a programme at a university accredited by the Commission for University Education (CUE). A Bachelor of Education (B.Ed) combines subject content with teaching methodology and is the most direct route. Alternatively, a subject degree (e.g., BSc Mathematics) combined with a Postgraduate Diploma in Education (PGDE) from a recognised institution qualifies the holder for that subject area. Minimum grade requirements — typically C+ in relevant subjects at KCSE — as set by KUCCPS apply at university entry.

02

Apply for TSC Registration

After graduating, apply for registration on the TSC online portal (tsc.go.ke). Required documents: certified academic transcripts and certificates, National ID or Passport, Certificate of Good Conduct from the DCI, two recent passport photographs, and the prescribed registration fee. TSC vets all applications and cross-checks with university records. Registration typically takes 30–90 days. Upon approval, a unique TSC Registration Number is issued — your permanent professional teacher identity used in all official employment, deployment, and payroll records.

03

Complete TSC Induction Programme

All newly registered teachers must complete the TSC Induction Programme before deployment. Induction covers: the Teachers Service Commission Act (2012), the Teachers Code of Regulations (2015), professional ethics and teacher conduct, CBC curriculum overview, lesson planning standards, the TPAD performance appraisal system, and mandatory child safeguarding policies. Induction is conducted at sub-county level by TSC Curriculum Support Officers (CSOs). A completion certificate is issued and is a prerequisite for receiving a school deployment letter.

04

Complete KICD CBC Retooling

KICD's CBC Retooling Programme equips in-service and new teachers with CBC-specific knowledge, skills, and pedagogical mindsets. Modules cover: CBC curriculum philosophy and the 2-6-3-3 structure, writing and interpreting learning outcomes (VOC, ABCD, SMART, Mager's models), using all 13 formative assessment tools, differentiated instruction, designing projects and rubrics, facilitating CSL, and ICT integration. Available as face-to-face workshops at KICD centres and County Training Centres, and as online self-paced modules via the KICD LMS portal. A retooling certificate is issued on completion. For senior secondary deployment from 2025, retooling certification is treated as mandatory.

05

Deployment, TPAD Annual Cycle & Licence Renewal

After induction and retooling, TSC issues a deployment letter assigning the teacher to a school and sub-county. Once deployed, the teacher enters the annual Teacher Professional Appraisal and Development (TPAD) cycle — self-appraisal, peer observation, and principal appraisal against CBC-aligned performance indicators. TPAD scores directly influence promotion, deployment transfers, and CPD planning. TSC teaching licences require annual renewal: teachers submit updated CPD records, current TPAD scores, and the renewal fee via the TSC portal. Failure to renew results in suspension from teaching duties and potential disciplinary proceedings.

// Documentation & Continuous Professional Development

Mandatory Documentation & CPD Obligations

Professional teaching under CBC requires more than classroom delivery. Teachers must maintain a comprehensive, current portfolio of professional documentation and engage in structured CPD throughout their careers.

📁 Mandatory Teacher Documentation Checklist

Every CBC teacher — in public and private schools — must maintain and produce these documents on demand during TSC, KICD, or Ministry quality assurance visits.

✓

TSC Registration Certificate

Valid and current. Renewed annually via the TSC portal. Should be displayed in the teacher's office or staffroom file.

✓

KICD CBC Retooling Certificate

Issued after completing KICD retooling (face-to-face or online). Should be updated every 3 years as new pathway modules are added.

✓

Scheme of Work

A term-long instructional plan covering all strands, sub-strands, and learning outcomes per the KICD curriculum design — with specific assessment tools referenced for each week.

✓

CBC-Format Lesson Plans

Must follow KICD's prescribed format: learning outcomes (VOC/ABCD), core competencies addressed, key vocabulary, learning activities, and selected assessment methods.

✓

Competency Assessment Records

Class registers recording each learner's performance on each competency indicator using CBC's four-level rating scale: Exceeding, Meeting, Approaching, or Below Expectation.

✓

CBC Learner Progress Report Cards

CBC uses competency-based report cards, not percentage marks. Teachers must correctly complete KICD-format report cards for every learner each term.

✓

Professional Teaching Portfolio

A personal portfolio documenting teaching reflections, sample learner work, assessment records, CPD certificates, and evidence of PBL and CSL activities conducted.

✓

Certificate of Good Conduct

Issued by the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI). Required for initial TSC registration and renewed every 5 years for continued child safeguarding compliance.

✓

CSL Project Records

Documentation of Community Service Learning activities: project brief, learner participation records, photographic evidence, and reflection reports from teacher and learners.

Continuous Professional Development (CPD)

CBC teachers are expected to engage in structured CPD throughout their careers. TSC tracks CPD compliance as part of annual TPAD assessment. CPD activities span formal training to peer learning and self-directed study.

🎓

KICD Subject-Specific Workshops

Periodic workshops organised by KICD at national and county level. These update teachers on new curriculum designs, emerging content areas, and revised assessment tools. Both attendance and demonstrated application are tracked.

Recognised CPD Activity
👥

Subject-Based Communities of Practice

KICD supports subject-based CoPs at school, zonal, sub-county, and county levels. Teachers meet at least once per term to share lesson plans, discuss assessment challenges, review learner work, and develop shared instructional resources.

Minimum 1× Per Term
💻

Online Self-Directed CPD

KICD's online learning portal offers self-paced CBC modules. Additional platforms recognised for teacher CPD include Coursera and edX for ICT integration, Khan Academy for content refresh, and subject-specific MOOC providers.

Ongoing · Self-Paced
🔬

Classroom Action Research

Structured classroom-based action research — identifying a teaching challenge, implementing a solution, and documenting outcomes — is encouraged. Findings can be shared in CoPs or submitted to KICD's Teacher Practice Repository.

TPAD-Valued Activity
📜

Postgraduate Study

A Master's in Education, Curriculum & Instruction, Educational Technology, or a relevant subject area qualifies as CPD. TSC offers a study leave policy for eligible teachers pursuing postgraduate qualifications that strengthen their CBC delivery.

TSC Study Leave Available
🤝

Peer Observation & Mentoring

Structured peer observation — one teacher observes another's lesson with a pre-agreed focus and provides constructive feedback — is among the most effective CBC CPD modes. Schools are encouraged to build formal peer observation schedules into their annual academic calendar.
