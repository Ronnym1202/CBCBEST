// 01 · Junior Secondary School

Junior Secondary Learning Areas (Grades 7–9)

Junior Secondary School (JSS) is the bridging phase between Upper Primary and Senior School. All learners — regardless of future pathway — study the same 12 compulsory learning areas from Grade 7 to Grade 9. The Kenya Junior School Education Assessment (KJSEA) at the end of Grade 9 informs Senior School pathway placement.

📋

All 12 JSS Learning Areas Are Compulsory

There are no electives at JSS level. Every learner studies the same 12 areas. Assessment is continuous (formative tools throughout each term) plus a summative KJSEA at Grade 9. KJSEA results guide — but do not restrict — Senior School pathway choice. Source: KICD Basic Education Curriculum Framework (BECF).

Grades 7 · 8 · 9 — All Compulsory

// 02 · Senior School — Core Subjects (Grades 10–12)

Core Subjects: Compulsory for Every Senior School Learner

Regardless of which pathway a learner selects — STEM, Social Sciences, or Arts & Sports Science — all Senior School learners at Grades 10, 11, and 12 must study the following four core subjects. These are prescribed by KICD and the Ministry of Education as compulsory for the Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE).

ℹ️

How Senior School Subject Selection Works

Each learner takes the 4 core subjects plus 3 elective subjects chosen from within their selected pathway (subject to school availability). This gives every learner 7 subjects in total. Additional weekly time is allocated to Physical Education, ICT Skills, Pastoral/PPI, and Personal/Group Study. See the lesson distribution table below.

📌
// All Pathways · Grades 10–12

Core Subjects (4) — All Learners

These four subjects are compulsory for every Senior School learner regardless of pathway selection. They form the foundation of the Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education (KCSE) and are allocated 5 + 5 + 5 + 3 = 18 lessons per week.

English

Core — All Pathways

Develops advanced reading, writing, oral communication, and literature competencies. English is the primary medium of instruction across all subjects and is therefore foundational to academic success at Senior School.

Key Content Areas
  • Language Use: Grammar, syntax, vocabulary development, register and style in academic and creative contexts
  • Reading & Comprehension: Unseen passages, inferencing, critical reading of print and digital texts
  • Writing: Essays, reports, letters, narratives, summaries; formal and informal registers
  • Oral Communication: Speeches, debates, interviews, presentations, listening skills
  • Literature: Set texts in prose, poetry, drama; analysis and response; Kenyan and African literature included
Essay writingCritical readingOral presentationsLiterary analysis

Assessment: Written papers (language and literature), oral examination component, continuous assessment tasks (CATs), KCSE Paper 1, 2 & 3.

Kiswahili / KSL

Core — All Pathways

Kenya's national and co-official language. Kiswahili is compulsory for all Senior School learners. Kenya Sign Language (KSL) is offered as an equivalent alternative for learners with hearing impairment, in line with the KICD inclusive education framework.

Key Content Areas
  • Fasihi (Literature): Riwaya, mashairi, tamthilia — Kenyan and East African Kiswahili literary works
  • Lugha (Language): Sarufi (grammar), uandishi, matumizi ya lugha, maneno ya kisasa
  • Mawasiliano: Mazungumzo, hotuba, uandishi wa barua rasmi na zisizo rasmi
  • KSL (where applicable): Sign vocabulary, grammar, conversational KSL, Deaf culture and community awareness

Assessment: Written papers, oral/aural component, CATs, KCSE Karatasi ya 1 (Lugha), Karatasi ya 2 (Fasihi).

Core Mathematics / Essential Mathematics

Core — All Pathways

All Senior School learners take either Core Mathematics (for STEM and more mathematically oriented learners) or Essential Mathematics (applied, contextualised mathematics for Social Sciences and Arts & Sports Science learners). Both are KCSE-examined.

Core Mathematics Content Areas (STEM-oriented)
  • Numbers & Algebra: Polynomials, surds, logarithms, indices, sequences & series, quadratic & simultaneous equations
  • Calculus: Differentiation, integration, rates of change, maxima & minima, applications in STEM contexts
  • Statistics & Probability: Data collection, frequency distributions, measures of central tendency & dispersion, probability models
  • Geometry & Trigonometry: Coordinate geometry, circles, transformations, sine & cosine rules
  • Financial Mathematics: Compound interest, depreciation, loan repayments, investment analysis
  • Vectors & Matrices: Operations, determinants, applications in geometry
Essential Mathematics Content Areas (Applied)
  • Number and operations in real-world contexts; percentages, ratios, proportional reasoning
  • Financial mathematics: budgeting, taxation, loans, savings, insurance
  • Basic statistics and data handling for social, arts and business contexts
  • Geometry for practical applications: measurements, scale, area, volume
Quantitative reasoningMathematical modellingProblem solvingData analysis
📌 Sample project (Core): Model population growth in a Kenyan county using exponential functions, presenting a 10-year projection with statistical analysis and graphical interpretation.

Assessment: CATs each term, written tests, mathematical investigations, KCSE Paper 1 & Paper 2.

Community Service Learning (CSL)

Core — All Pathways

Community Service Learning is a structured, credit-bearing learning experience that integrates meaningful community service with instruction and reflection. It is compulsory for all Senior School learners and contributes to the KCSE score. CSL is allocated 3 lessons per week.

Key Content Areas
  • Community Engagement: Identifying community needs, designing service projects, implementation and documentation
  • Civic Responsibility: Understanding rights and responsibilities, active citizenship, national values
  • Reflection: Journal writing, portfolio documentation, connecting service to curriculum learning
  • Leadership & Teamwork: Group project management, communication, conflict resolution
  • Environmental Stewardship: Sustainability projects, conservation activities, waste management initiatives
Civic engagementLeadershipReflection writingTeamwork
📌 Sample project: Organise and implement a school environmental project (e.g., tree planting, waste sorting programme), documenting planning, execution, impact assessment, and personal reflection in a CSL portfolio.

Assessment: CSL portfolio (community service log, reflective journal, project reports), observation by teacher/community supervisor, oral presentation, KCSE portfolio submission.

Additional Non-Examined Provision

In addition to the subjects above, the KICD framework stipulates that: (a) Physical Education (PE) and ICT Skills will be offered to all learners to facilitate learning, enjoyment and acquisition of life skills; (b) every school shall offer a Pastoral/Religious Programme of Instruction (P/PRI) to enhance moral, spiritual and character development; and (c) there shall be one lesson per week for personal or group study. These are allocated weekly lesson time but are not pathway electives.

// 04 · STEM Pathway — Elective Subjects

STEM Pathway: Elective Subjects

Learners in the STEM pathway choose 3 elective subjects from the list below in addition to the 4 core subjects. Elective availability depends on school capacity, qualified teacher availability, and infrastructure. Subject combinations are guided by school and KICD recommendations.

🔬
// Senior School Pathway 01 · STEM

Science, Technology, Engineering & Mathematics

Prepares learners for careers in scientific research, technology, medicine, engineering, agriculture, and data-driven industries. Emphasises inquiry-based learning, laboratory practice, computational thinking, and mathematical modelling.

ELECTIVE Learner selects 3 from the STEM subject list — subject to school availability

Biology

STEM Elective

Explores living systems at the cellular, organismal, and ecosystem level. Develops scientific inquiry, laboratory techniques, and understanding of life processes relevant to medicine, agriculture, and environmental management.

Key Content Areas
  • Cell Biology: Cell structure, cell division (mitosis & meiosis), transport mechanisms
  • Genetics & Heredity: DNA structure, Mendelian genetics, mutation, genetic engineering, biotechnology
  • Human Physiology: Nutrition, transport, respiration, excretion, reproduction, nervous & endocrine systems
  • Ecology & Environment: Food webs, nutrient cycles, population dynamics, conservation, climate change
  • Microbiology & Immunology: Microorganisms, disease causation, vaccines, antibiotics
  • Plant Biology: Photosynthesis, plant transport, growth hormones, reproduction
Lab techniquesScientific methodMicroscopyDissection
📌 Sample project: Investigate the effect of salt concentration on osmosis in plant cells, present findings using graphs and a formal scientific report.

Assessment: Practical lab reports, written tests, CATs, KCSE Paper 1 (theory) & Paper 2 (practical/essay).

Chemistry

STEM Elective

Develops deep understanding of matter, its properties, reactions, and applications. Practical chemistry is central — learners spend significant time in labs performing titrations, preparations, and qualitative analysis.

Key Content Areas
  • Atomic Structure & Periodicity: Electronic configuration, periodic trends, chemical bonding
  • Stoichiometry: Mole concept, balancing equations, yield calculations, empirical formulae
  • Organic Chemistry: Hydrocarbons, functional groups, reactions, polymers, petrochemicals
  • Thermochemistry: Enthalpy changes, Hess's Law, bond energies, energy profiles
  • Electrochemistry: Electrolysis, galvanic cells, corrosion, applications
  • Industrial Chemistry: Haber process, Contact process, water treatment, polymer manufacture
TitrationChemical safetyQualitative analysisReport writing
📌 Sample project: Compare water hardness from three local sources using a soap test and EDTA titration, then propose cost-effective treatment options.

Assessment: Practical lab reports, written tests, CATs, KCSE Paper 1 (theory) & Paper 2 (practical).

Physics

STEM Elective

Explores the fundamental laws governing matter and energy. Develops engineering intuition, measurement precision, and analytical skills underpinning all physical technology — from electronics to aviation and renewable energy.

Key Content Areas
  • Mechanics: Motion, Newton's Laws, momentum, energy, circular motion, simple machines
  • Electricity & Magnetism: Circuits, electromagnetic induction, AC/DC, motors & generators
  • Waves & Optics: Wave properties, reflection, refraction, diffraction, lenses, mirrors, sound, light
  • Thermodynamics: Heat transfer, thermal expansion, gas laws, kinetic theory
  • Modern Physics: Radioactivity, nuclear reactions, photoelectric effect, X-rays
  • Electronics: Semiconductors, diodes, transistors, logic gates
Circuit buildingMeasurement precisionGraphical analysisError analysis
📌 Sample project: Construct a simple DC electric motor from recycled materials, measure voltage output and RPM, and relate findings to electromagnetic induction principles.

Assessment: Practical experiments, written tests, CATs, KCSE Paper 1 & Paper 2 (practical).

General Science

STEM Elective

An integrated science elective offering a broader coverage of biology, chemistry, and physics concepts for learners not specialising in a single science. Particularly relevant for learners targeting health sciences, environmental studies, or teaching.

Key Content Areas
  • Integrated life sciences: cells, body systems, ecology and environment
  • Physical sciences: matter, forces, energy, waves
  • Earth and space science: geology, climate, astronomy basics
  • Science, technology and society: the role of science in Kenya's development

Assessment: Written tests, practical work, CATs, KCSE papers.

Agriculture

STEM Elective

Applies scientific knowledge to farming systems, land management, food security, and agribusiness. Highly relevant to Kenya's agricultural economy and livelihoods across all 47 counties.

Key Content Areas
  • Crop Production: Soil science, planting systems, pest & disease management, irrigation, post-harvest handling
  • Animal Production: Livestock breeds, nutrition, health management, dairy & poultry production
  • Agribusiness: Farm records, value chain analysis, agricultural marketing, financial planning
  • Soil & Water Management: Soil testing, conservation, water harvesting, hydroponics, agroforestry
📌 Sample project: Set up a drip irrigation vegetable plot, monitor growth under different watering regimes, and present a cost-benefit analysis of the harvest.

Computer Studies

STEM Elective

Prepares learners for Kenya's digital economy. Covers programming, data structures, networks, databases, cybersecurity, and emerging technologies — aligned with the Kenya ICT Authority's digital transformation agenda.

Key Content Areas
  • Programming & Algorithms: Python/JavaScript, control structures, functions, recursion, sorting & searching
  • Data & Databases: SQL, database design, data validation, spreadsheet modelling
  • Computer Systems: Hardware, operating systems, memory management, binary representation
  • Networks & Internet: Network topologies, protocols (TCP/IP, HTTP), cloud computing, IoT
  • Cybersecurity & Ethics: Digital security, privacy, encryption basics, Kenya's cybercrime laws
  • Emerging Technologies: AI basics, machine learning concepts, robotics, mobile development
PythonComputational thinkingDebuggingDatabase design
📌 Sample project: Design and deploy a simple school information system with a database, query interface, and web-based front end — fully documented.

Home Science

STEM Elective

Applies biology, chemistry, and nutrition science to food technology, textile science, and household management. Strongly linked to hospitality, dietetics, consumer science, and early childhood development careers.

Key Content Areas
  • Food science: nutrients, food processing, preservation, food safety and labelling
  • Textile science: fibres, fabric care, pattern drafting, garment construction, Kenyan textile traditions
  • Consumer education: budgeting, household management, environmental sustainability
  • Child development and family health

Aviation

STEM Elective

An innovative STEM elective covering the science and technology of flight, aerospace systems, meteorology, and aviation safety. Prepares learners for careers in commercial aviation, aerospace engineering, and Kenya's growing aviation sector.

Key Content Areas
  • Principles of flight: aerodynamics, lift, drag, thrust
  • Aircraft systems and navigation basics
  • Meteorology and weather interpretation for aviation
  • Aviation safety, regulations, and KCAA framework

Technical Subjects

STEM Elective

A cluster of technical and applied STEM electives available where schools have appropriate workshops and qualified teachers. Learners choose one: Building Construction, Electricity, Metalwork, Power Mechanics, Wood Technology, Marine & Fisheries Technology, or Media Technology.

Available Technical Electives
  • Building Construction: Masonry, carpentry, plumbing, drawing, site safety
  • Electricity: Circuit theory, installation, electrical safety, renewable energy systems
  • Metalwork: Metal properties, fabrication, welding, machining, design
  • Power Mechanics: Internal combustion engines, automotive systems, servicing
  • Wood Technology: Timber properties, joinery, furniture making, finishing
  • Media Technology: Print, digital, broadcast media production and design
  • Marine & Fisheries Technology: Marine ecosystems, fish farming, boat handling, aquaculture

Social Sciences Pathway: Elective Subjects

Learners in the Social Sciences pathway choose 3 elective subjects from the list below. This pathway cultivates responsible citizens, critical thinkers, and community leaders suited for careers in law, public administration, education, journalism, NGO leadership, and diplomacy.

⚖️
// Senior School Pathway 02 · Social Sciences

Social Sciences Pathway

Humanities, languages, civic education, and social studies. Develops analytical, communicative, and civic competencies for careers in law, public administration, education, diplomacy, journalism, and social development.

ELECTIVE Learner selects 3 from the Social Sciences subject list

History and Citizenship

Social Sciences Elective

Develops historical consciousness, civic awareness, and understanding of how governments and societies evolve. Encourages critical reading of sources, debate, and active citizenship. Note: This subject replaces the former "History & Government" title under CBC.

Key Content Areas
  • Kenyan History: Pre-colonial societies, colonialism, independence movements, post-independence development, devolution
  • African History: Pre-colonial civilisations, Trans-Atlantic slave trade, colonisation of Africa, liberation movements, AU
  • World History: World Wars, Cold War, United Nations, globalisation, international relations
  • Citizenship & Governance: Kenya's Constitution 2010, governance structures, human rights, democratic participation, devolved government
  • Source Analysis: Primary and secondary sources, bias detection, oral history, document analysis
Source analysisCritical thinkingEssay writingCivic engagement
📌 Sample project: Conduct oral history interviews with community elders about colonial land policies, then compile a report connecting findings to post-independence land reform.

Geography

Social Sciences Elective

Analyses the physical and human world — landforms, climate, population, settlement, and resource management. Fieldwork and GIS tools bring geography to life in real Kenyan environments.

Key Content Areas
  • Physical Geography: Plate tectonics, weathering, drainage systems, climatic zones, soils
  • Human Geography: Population, migration, urbanisation, settlement patterns, economic activities
  • Environmental Geography: Deforestation, desertification, water scarcity, climate change impacts in East Africa
  • Map Work & Fieldwork: Topographic maps, aerial photos, GIS basics, transect surveys
  • Kenya's Physical & Economic Geography: Landforms, rivers, lakes, highlands, agriculture zones, tourism
Map readingFieldworkGIS basicsData analysis
📌 Sample project: Conduct a transect fieldwork study along a local river, identify erosion and deposition signs, and propose community conservation strategies with annotated maps.

Business Studies

Social Sciences Elective

Develops entrepreneurial thinking, financial literacy, marketing skills, and understanding of how businesses operate in Kenya's economy — directly applicable to SME development and formal commerce careers.

Key Content Areas
  • Entrepreneurship: Business planning, innovation, start-up financing, social enterprise models
  • Accounting: Double-entry bookkeeping, trial balance, income statements, balance sheets, cash flow
  • Marketing: Market research, consumer behaviour, pricing, promotion, distribution channels
  • Business Environment: Types of business organisations, business ethics, labour relations, taxation in Kenya
  • ICT in Business: Spreadsheet modelling, e-commerce, digital marketing, mobile money systems (M-Pesa)
AccountingBusiness planningFinancial literacyMarket research
📌 Sample project: Develop a full business plan for a community-based enterprise including market analysis, startup costs, projected income statement, and pitch presentation.

Christian Religious Education (CRE)

Social Sciences Elective

Develops ethical reasoning, moral values, and understanding of the Christian faith in the context of Kenyan society. Encourages respect for religious diversity and application of Christian values to contemporary issues.

Key Content Areas
  • Old and New Testament study: themes, context, significance
  • Christian ethics and moral decision-making
  • The church in Kenya: history, role in society, ecumenism
  • Contemporary moral issues: justice, human rights, environmental stewardship

Islamic Religious Education (IRE)

Social Sciences Elective

Develops knowledge of Islamic principles, moral conduct, and the history of Islam in East Africa. Encourages ethical reasoning and responsible citizenship grounded in Islamic values.

Key Content Areas
  • Quranic studies, Hadith, Islamic jurisprudence (Fiqh)
  • Moral conduct, Islamic ethics and character development
  • Islamic civilisation in East Africa and Kenya
  • Contemporary issues from an Islamic perspective

Hindu Religious Education (HRE)

Social Sciences Elective

Introduces Hindu scripture, dharma, ethics, and the contributions of Hindu traditions to Kenyan culture and society.

Key Content Areas
  • Hindu scriptures, Dharma and ethical principles
  • Rituals, festivals and their significance
  • Hindu contributions to Kenyan history and culture
  • Interfaith dialogue and contemporary social issues

Literature in English

Social Sciences Elective

An advanced literary studies elective going beyond the core English component. Focuses on critical literary analysis, Kenyan and African literature, and the social contexts of texts.

Key Content Areas
  • Prose, poetry, drama — Kenyan, African, and world literature
  • Literary theory and criticism; context and meaning
  • Oral literature traditions in Kenya
  • Creative writing: narrative, poetry, scriptwriting

Fasihi ya Kiswahili

Social Sciences Elective

Advanced Kiswahili literary studies as a standalone elective. Develops deep engagement with Kiswahili prose, poetry, and drama across Kenyan and East African literary traditions.

Key Content Areas
  • Riwaya (novels), mashairi (poetry), tamthilia (drama) — set texts and unseen
  • Fasihi simulizi: hadithi, methali, vitendawili, ngano
  • Literary criticism and analysis in Kiswahili
  • Uandishi wa ubunifu (creative writing in Kiswahili)

Foreign Languages

Social Sciences Elective

Learners may take one foreign language as a Social Sciences elective. Available options (subject to school staffing) include Arabic, French, German, and Mandarin Chinese.

Available Foreign Languages
  • Arabic: Conversational, reading, writing, and cultural study; aligned to Kenya's East African trade connections
  • French: Communication skills, grammar, culture; useful for EAC, AU, and international careers
  • German: Language skills and German-speaking world culture
  • Mandarin Chinese: Communication, writing, and Chinese culture; aligned to Kenya-China bilateral relations

Indigenous Languages

Social Sciences Elective

Preserves and promotes Kenya's rich linguistic heritage by offering indigenous Kenyan languages as formal elective subjects for study and KCSE examination.

Key Content Areas
  • Language structure, grammar and vocabulary of selected indigenous language
  • Oral literature, proverbs, riddles and folk tales
  • Cultural practices and their expression through language
  • Written communication in the indigenous language

Sign Language

Social Sciences Elective

Kenya Sign Language as a full elective subject, enabling all learners — not just those with hearing impairment — to develop KSL proficiency and promote inclusive communication in Kenyan society.

Key Content Areas
  • KSL vocabulary and grammatical structure
  • Deaf culture, identity and community in Kenya
  • Conversational and academic KSL use
  • Interpreting and inclusive communication skills

// 07 · Cross-Cutting Core Competencies

The 7 CBC Core Competencies: Assessed Across All Subjects

The seven CBC core competencies are not subject-specific — they run across all learning areas and all pathways from Grade 7 to Grade 12. Every subject teacher is responsible for deliberately developing and assessing these competencies through daily learning activities, projects, and community engagement tasks as specified in the KICD curriculum designs.

Competency How it appears in subjects Typical assessment method
Communication & Collaboration Group projects, oral presentations, peer reviews, debate, collaborative science lab reports Observation schedule, peer assessment, oral Q&A, scoring rubric
Critical Thinking & Problem Solving Mathematical modelling, source analysis in History, case studies in Business Studies, chemistry experiments Written tests, project assessment, anecdotal records, rubric
Creativity & Imagination Art portfolios, technology design, music composition, drama writing, innovative STEM solutions Portfolio, project, rating scale, public exhibition
Citizenship Community Service Learning (CSL) projects, History debates, Religious Education ethics, Geography conservation fieldwork Community service log, reflection journals, presentations, interviews
Digital Literacy Computer Studies, digital art tools, Business Studies spreadsheets, research using ICT, film production Practical exam, project, portfolio of digital work
Learning to Learn Self-assessment tasks, portfolio reflection, independent research, study skills across all subjects Self-assessment, portfolio, learning logs, checklists

Common Subjects & Cross-Pathway Provision

All Senior School learners — regardless of pathway — must study English, Kiswahili/KSL, Core/Essential Mathematics, and Community Service Learning (CSL) throughout Grades 10–12. These are examined in the KCSE alongside the three pathway elective subjects. Physical Education, ICT Skills, PPI, and Personal/Group Study time are provided weekly to all learners as non-examined provision.

// 08 · For Teachers

Delivering CBC Subjects Effectively

Each subject in CBC demands a specific pedagogical approach. Below are the key requirements every CBC teacher must fulfil when delivering any learning area — as per KICD curriculum designs and TSC Teacher Standards Framework.

📋

Scheme of Work & Lesson Plans

Teachers must prepare termly schemes of work and individual lesson plans for every topic. Plans specify the learning outcome, teaching/learning activities, resources, and assessment method. CBC lesson plans follow a learner-centred format — active learning, not lecture-style delivery.

📊

Formative Assessment Records

All 13 KICD assessment tools must be applied appropriately throughout the term. Teachers maintain individual learner records, track progress against strand learning outcomes, and give timely, specific feedback. Continuous assessment contributes 60% of the final KCSE grade.

🏗️

Project-Based Learning (PBL)

Each learning area must include project work connecting subject content to real-world, community-relevant contexts. Projects should be authentic and allow learners to apply multiple competencies. Teachers facilitate rather than direct, allowing genuine learner agency and initiative.

🧩

Differentiated Instruction

CBC requires teachers to address diverse learning needs within the same classroom. Content, process, and product must be adapted for learners with different abilities, learning styles, and needs — including learners with special needs, gifted learners, and those requiring remediation.

💻

ICT Integration

All teachers must integrate ICT into their teaching — using digital resources, online research, subject-specific software, and eLearning platforms where available. This is especially required for STEM and Computer Studies teachers but applies across all pathways per TSC standards.

📁

Learner Portfolio Management

Teachers guide learners in building subject portfolios documenting progress, projects, and reflections. Portfolio assessment is one of the 13 KICD tools and contributes to the continuous assessment score. Portfolios are reviewed by learners, teachers, and external assessors.
