// 01 · What is CBC

Understanding Kenya's Competency Based Curriculum

CBC replaces the 8-4-4 system with a learner-centred, skills-focused approach. It emphasises what a learner can do — not just what they know.

🎯

What CBC focuses on

CBC shifts from content memorisation to competency development. Learning is project-based, continuous, and tied to real-life application. Formative assessments — not just end-of-year exams — determine progress.

  • Learner-centred pedagogy
  • Community Service Learning (CSL)
  • Project-Based Learning (PBL)
  • Continuous Assessment Tests (CATs)
  • Portfolio-based evidence of learning
  • 60% coursework, 40% external exams

🏫 Junior Secondary (Grades 7–9)

Core subjects: English, Kiswahili, Mathematics, Integrated Science, Health Education, Social Studies, Religious Education, Creative Arts, Sports & Physical Education. Learners sit the Kenya Junior School Education Assessment (KJSEA) at the end of Grade 9 to inform pathway placement.

🎓 Senior Secondary (Grades 10–12)

Learners choose one of three pathways based on aptitude, interests, and KJSEA results. Each pathway includes core (compulsory) subjects plus electives. Projects, work-based learning, and community service are central. From 2025/2026, the first cohort enters senior school — pathway selection is now critical.

// 03 · Core Competencies

The 7 Core Competencies

CBC is built around seven competencies every Kenyan learner must develop across all learning areas and grade levels.

01
Communication & Collaboration
Ability to communicate effectively and work in teams toward shared goals.
02
Critical Thinking & Problem Solving
Applying reason and evidence to analyse situations and develop solutions.
03
Creativity & Imagination
Generating novel ideas, inventions, and approaches to real challenges.
04
Citizenship
Understanding rights, responsibilities, and active participation in society.
05
Digital Literacy
Using technology responsibly and effectively for learning and communication.
06
Learning to Learn
Developing self-directed habits, metacognition, and lifelong learning skills.
07
Self-Efficacy
Belief in one's ability to succeed and regulate personal growth.

// 05 · KICD Assessment Tools

All 13 CBC Assessment Tools

KICD prescribes 13 assessment instruments for measuring learner competencies in formative and summative contexts. Teachers select tools based on learning area, strand, and grade level.

01

Observation Schedule

Structured tool for recording specific learner behaviours and skills during activities in real time.

02

Oral Questions & Answers

Verbal assessment of learner understanding through direct questioning and responses during lessons.

03

Written Tests

Pen-and-paper assessments measuring cognitive recall, comprehension, and application of concepts.

04

Checklist

A list of criteria or skills used to record whether a learner has demonstrated expected competencies.

05

Rating Scale

A scale (e.g. 1–4 or Excellent/Good/Fair) used to rate the degree to which a learner meets a criterion.

06

Rubric

A detailed scoring guide describing performance levels for each criterion — both analytic and holistic types are used in CBC.

07

Portfolio

A collection of a learner's work over time, showing growth, effort, and achievement toward learning outcomes.

08

Project

Extended tasks requiring learners to apply knowledge and skills to produce a tangible product or investigation.

09

Anecdotal Records

Brief, factual notes written by teachers about significant observations of individual learner behaviour or performance.

10

Peer Assessment

Learners evaluate each other's work using agreed criteria, developing critical evaluation and communication skills.

11

Self-Assessment

Learners reflect on and evaluate their own performance, fostering metacognition and ownership of learning.

12

Questionnaire

A structured set of written questions used to gather learner attitudes, perceptions, or knowledge.

13

Interview

A one-on-one or group verbal exchange between teacher and learner(s) to assess depth of understanding.

// 06 · Learning Outcome Models

Writing Expected Learning Outcomes

CBC requires teachers to write clear, measurable learning outcomes. Four KICD-recognised models guide this process — each ensuring outcomes are learner-focused, observable, and achievable.

Model 1

VOC Model

The simplest and most widely used model in CBC. A learning outcome consists of three elements joined into one sentence.

VVerb
OObject
CContext
Verb: Action word from Bloom's Taxonomy (e.g. Identify, Explain, Design)
Object: The content or concept being learned
Context: The condition or situation in which learning occurs

📌 "Identify types of soil in the local environment"
Model 2

ABCD Model

A four-component model that adds audience and performance degree. Widely used in instructional design and CBC curriculum planning.

AAudience
BBehaviour
CCondition
DDegree
A: Who (the learner)
B: What they will do (observable action)
C: Under what circumstances
D: How well / to what standard

📌 "The learner will solve quadratic equations using the formula with 80% accuracy"
Model 3

SMART Model

Widely used in goal-setting and lesson planning to ensure outcomes are realistic and time-bound.

SSpecific
MMeasurable
AAchievable
RRelevant
TTime-bound
Each outcome must be clearly stated, measurable by assessment, realistic for the learner's level, relevant to the CBC strand, and achievable within the lesson or unit period.

📌 "By end of lesson, learners will correctly label 5 parts of a plant cell on a diagram"
Model 4

Mager's Performance Model

Developed by Robert Mager — focuses on observable performance, conditions, and measurable criterion standards.

PPerformance
CCondition
CrCriterion
Performance: What the learner will do (observable)
Condition: The given resources, constraints, or context
Criterion: The standard of acceptable performance

📌 "Given a map, identify 4 physical features with correct labels and coordinates"

// 07 · Teacher Standards

Professional Teacher Requirements

CBC demands a new kind of teacher — one who facilitates, assesses continuously, integrates ICT, and supports each learner's individual pathway.

👩‍🏫

CBC teacher competency standards (KICD & TSC)

To deliver quality CBC, a teacher must hold subject-specific qualifications, TSC registration, and demonstrated competency in formative assessment, learner-centred pedagogies, differentiated instruction, and ICT integration. For instance, a STEM Physics teacher requires a Bachelor's degree in Physics Education; an Arts & Sports instructor needs proven practical expertise and coaching certification.

All teachers must also complete TSC-mandated CBC retooling, maintain CPD hours, and demonstrate proficiency in the 13 KICD assessment tools.
