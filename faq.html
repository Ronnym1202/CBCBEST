// Topic 01

General CBC Questions

10 Q&As

CBC — the Competency Based Curriculum — is Kenya's reformed national education system, introduced to replace the 8-4-4 model. Rather than rewarding rote memorisation and performance in a single high-stakes examination, CBC evaluates what a learner can actually do with knowledge: apply it, analyse it, create with it, and use it to solve real-world problems.

CBC was designed based on the recommendations of the Task Force on the Re-Alignment of Education to the Constitution (Odhiambo Report, 2012) and the Basic Education Curriculum Framework (BECF, 2017), which were developed to align education with Kenya's Vision 2030 and the East African Community integration goals.

Core purpose: To develop well-rounded, globally competitive Kenyan citizens with skills in critical thinking, creativity, digital literacy, communication, and self-directed learning.
📌 Source: KICD Basic Education Curriculum Framework (BECF), 2017

The differences span structure, philosophy, assessment, and learning approach:

  • Structure: 8-4-4 ran for 16 years (8 primary, 4 secondary, 4 university minimum). CBC follows a 2-6-3-3 structure (2 pre-primary, 6 primary, 3 junior secondary, 3 senior secondary) before university or TVET.
  • Assessment: 8-4-4 relied on one high-stakes exam at each exit point (KCPE, KCSE). CBC uses continuous assessment (coursework contributes significantly) plus national examinations at transition points.
  • Pathways: 8-4-4 had one secondary school track for all. CBC offers three distinct senior school pathways aligned to learner aptitude and interest: STEM, Social Sciences, and Arts & Sports Science.
  • Focus: 8-4-4 was content-heavy and exam-focused. CBC emphasises the development of seven core competencies alongside subject knowledge.
  • Role of the teacher: In 8-4-4, teachers were primarily content transmitters. In CBC, teachers are facilitators of learning, mentors, and formative assessors.
  • Community engagement: CBC introduces Community Service Learning (CSL) as a mandatory component — not present in 8-4-4.
📌 Source: KICD BECF, 2017; KICD Curriculum Designs, 2022–2025

The BECF identifies seven core competencies that every Kenyan learner must develop across all learning areas and grade levels. These are embedded in every subject and assessed continuously:

  1. Communication and Collaboration — Effective expression of ideas and working productively in diverse groups.
  2. Critical Thinking and Problem Solving — Applying reasoning, evidence, and systematic approaches to analyse and resolve challenges.
  3. Creativity and Imagination — Generating original ideas, inventive solutions, and novel approaches to real problems.
  4. Citizenship — Understanding civic rights, national identity, responsibilities to society, and ethical conduct.
  5. Digital Literacy — Responsible, effective use of technology and digital tools for learning and communication.
  6. Learning to Learn — Developing self-directed study skills, metacognition, and habits of lifelong learning.
  7. Self-Efficacy — Belief in one's capacity to achieve, regulate personal growth, and persist through challenges.
These competencies are not separate subjects — they are woven into every lesson, project, and assessment across all learning areas from Grade 1 to Grade 12.
📌 Source: KICD BECF, 2017, Chapter 4

CBC was developed based on the 2012 Odhiambo Task Force recommendations. The key milestones are:

  • 2017: KICD publishes the Basic Education Curriculum Framework (BECF). Pilot schools begin phased trials.
  • 2019: Full national rollout begins with Grades 1, 2, and 3 simultaneously.
  • 2020–2023: Grade-by-grade rollout continues upward. COVID-19 caused delays and accelerated review of implementation guidelines.
  • 2023: First cohort completes Grade 6 and transitions to Junior Secondary School (Grade 7) — a landmark transition.
  • 2025/2026: First cohort enters Senior Secondary School (Grade 10) and selects a pathway.
  • Approx. 2028/2029: First CBC cohort sits for KCSE under the new framework.
📌 Source: MoE Implementation Guidelines; KICD Rollout Schedules

The Basic Education Curriculum Framework (BECF, 2017) is the foundational policy document that defines the entire philosophy, structure, and guiding principles of CBC in Kenya. It was developed by KICD and approved by the Ministry of Education.

The BECF sets out:

  • The 2-6-3-3 education structure
  • The 7 core competencies every learner must develop
  • The 6 core values (Unity, Responsibility, Respect, Integrity, Patriotism, Social Justice)
  • The 4 Pertinent and Contemporary Issues (PCI) embedded across all subjects
  • The general principles guiding curriculum design, assessment, and pedagogy
  • Roles of teachers, parents, community, and government in curriculum delivery

All KICD curriculum designs for individual subjects and grades must align with the BECF.

📌 Source: KICD BECF, 2017 — available at kicd.ac.ke

The BECF identifies six core values that CBC aims to instil in all learners:

  • Love
  • Responsibility
  • Respect
  • Unity
  • Peace
  • Patriotism

The BECF also identifies Pertinent and Contemporary Issues (PCIs) that must be embedded across all subjects — not taught as standalone topics, but integrated within lessons and projects. These include:

  • Environmental education
  • Health education (including HIV/AIDS)
  • Financial literacy
  • Substance abuse education
  • Safety and security
  • Child rights and responsibilities
  • Citizenship
  • Gender and social inclusion
📌 Source: KICD BECF, 2017, Chapter 5

Project-Based Learning (PBL) is a pedagogical strategy in CBC where learners engage in extended, real-world tasks that require applying knowledge from multiple subjects. Projects are assessed using rubrics and contribute to coursework grades. A mathematics project, for example, might involve collecting local data, computing statistics, and presenting findings to the school community.

Community Service Learning (CSL) is a mandatory component in which learners engage in organised service to their communities — such as environmental cleanup, visiting the elderly, or peer tutoring — and then reflect on the learning gained from that experience. CSL develops citizenship, empathy, and the competency of self-efficacy.

Both PBL and CSL are assessed and contribute to the School-Based Assessment (SBA) component of a learner's overall grade.
📌 Source: KICD BECF, 2017; KICD Assessment Policy, 2018

CBC covers all levels from Pre-Primary (PP1 and PP2) through Grade 12. As of the 2025/2026 academic year:

  • PP1–PP2: All learners in pre-primary are in CBC
  • Grades 1–9: All learners are fully in CBC (the first CBC cohort completed Grade 9/KJSEA in 2024)
  • Grade 10: The pioneer CBC senior school class enrolled in 2025
  • Grades 11–12: Will be fully in CBC by 2026 and 2027 respectively
  • Former 8-4-4 learners: Those still in Form 3–4 as of 2025 are completing 8-4-4 on a parallel track until the full transition is complete
📌 Source: MoE Transition Guidelines, 2023–2025

CBC has three national assessments at key transition points, all administered by KNEC (Kenya National Examinations Council):

  • KPSEA — Kenya Primary School Education Assessment: Sat at the end of Grade 6. Certifies completion of primary school and informs placement into Junior Secondary School. Not primarily a selection exam — all learners proceed to JSS.
  • KJSEA — Kenya Junior School Education Assessment: Sat at the end of Grade 9. Certifies junior secondary completion and is a key factor — alongside school-based aptitude assessment and guidance — in determining which senior secondary pathway a learner is placed into (STEM, Social Sciences, or Arts & Sports Science).
  • KCSE — Kenya Certificate of Secondary Education: Sat at the end of Grade 12. The terminal certificate for senior secondary school, used for university and TVET admissions. The first CBC cohort is expected to sit KCSE approximately 2028/2029.
📌 Source: KNEC; MoE CBC Assessment Policy

The MoE and KICD have publicly acknowledged several implementation challenges, and academic and professional bodies have raised concerns:

  • Infrastructure: Many schools lack the laboratories, libraries, ICT equipment, and materials needed for project-based, practical learning — particularly in rural and ASAL areas.
  • Teacher capacity: Not all teachers have received CBC retooling. Some teachers trained under 8-4-4 pedagogy find the shift to facilitation-based teaching difficult.
  • Assessment workload: The continuous assessment model, with 13 tools and multiple portfolios, creates a significant administrative burden for teachers who may have 40+ learners per class.
  • Pathway resources: Developing subject-specific resources (textbooks, lab equipment, ICT tools) for three distinct pathways requires considerably more investment than a single-track system.
  • Transition management: Running CBC and 8-4-4 simultaneously during the transition period has strained school administration.

The government has responded with phased textbook procurement, KICD digital resource platforms, and expanded TSC recruitment — though critics argue the pace is insufficient.

📌 Source: Kenya Education Review Commission (KERC), 2023; MoE Status Reports

The 2-6-3-3 Curriculum Structure

8 Q&As

The 2-6-3-3 refers to the number of years at each stage of Kenya's education system under CBC:

  • 2 years: Pre-Primary Education (PP1 and PP2) — ages approximately 4–5.
  • 6 years: Primary School (Grades 1–6) — ends with KPSEA.
  • 3 years: Junior Secondary School (Grades 7–9) — ends with KJSEA.
  • 3 years: Senior Secondary School (Grades 10–12) — ends with KCSE.

After KCSE, learners proceed to university (minimum 3–5 years), TVET programmes, or direct employment. Some describe this as 2-6-3-3-3 to include university.

📌 Source: KICD BECF, 2017

All learners in Grades 7–9 study the same core subjects — there are no pathways or options at this level. The Junior Secondary curriculum includes:

  • English
  • Kiswahili / Kenya Sign Language (for learners with hearing impairments)
  • Mathematics
  • Integrated Science
  • Health Education
  • Pre-Technical and Pre-Career Education
  • Social Studies
  • Religious Education (CRE, IRE, or HRE — one selected based on school/parent preference)
  • Business Studies
  • Agriculture
  • Home Science
  • Creative Arts and Design
  • Physical and Health Education (PHE)
  • Foreign Language (Arabic, French, German, Mandarin — where available)
JSS is designed to be broad and exploratory — helping learners discover their strengths before choosing a pathway at Grade 10.
📌 Source: KICD JSS Curriculum Designs, 2022–2023

Senior Secondary School runs for 3 years (Grades 10, 11, 12). Unlike JSS, it is organised into three distinct pathways:

  • STEM Pathway — focused on Sciences, Mathematics, Technology, and Engineering disciplines.
  • Social Sciences Pathway — focused on Humanities, Business, and Social Studies disciplines.
  • Arts and Sports Science Pathway — focused on Creative Arts, Performing Arts, Visual Design, and Sports Sciences.

Within each pathway, some subjects are compulsory for all (English, Kiswahili, Mathematics for STEM, PHE) and others are elective (chosen from within the pathway's subject cluster).

Assessment in SSS combines School-Based Assessment (SBA) and the KCSE national examination at Grade 12.

📌 Source: KICD SSS Curriculum Designs, 2024–2025; MoE SSS Policy

Primary school in CBC covers Grades 1 to 6, after which learners sit the KPSEA. The learning areas are:

  • English Activity / English (literacy, communication, and reading)
  • Kiswahili Activity / Kiswahili (Lugha ya Kiswahili)
  • Mathematical Activity / Mathematics
  • Environmental Activity / Science and Technology (lower primary); Integrated Science (upper primary)
  • Creative Arts (Arts & Craft, Music)
  • Physical and Health Education (PHE)
  • Religious Education (CRE, IRE, or HRE)
  • Home Science (upper primary)
  • Agriculture (upper primary)

Lower Primary (Grades 1–3) uses a more play-based, activity-centred approach. Upper Primary (Grades 4–6) introduces more formal disciplinary learning.

📌 Source: KICD Primary School Curriculum Designs, 2019–2022

Pre-Primary education in CBC covers two years — PP1 (age ~4) and PP2 (age ~5). Learning is entirely play-based, activity-centred, and exploratory. The aim is to develop foundational literacy, numeracy, and social-emotional skills rather than formal academic content.

Learning areas at this level include: Language Activities, Mathematical Activities, Environmental Activities, Psychomotor and Creative Activities, and Religious Activities. No formal examinations are administered at this stage.

📌 Source: KICD Pre-Primary Curriculum Design, 2019

TVET (Technical and Vocational Education and Training) is a recognised and equal destination after Senior Secondary School (Grade 12). It is not a second-tier option — CBC explicitly positions TVET alongside university as a valid pathway to skilled, productive careers.

Learners who sit KCSE may proceed to:

  • National Polytechnics (certificates, diplomas)
  • Technical Training Institutes (TTIs)
  • TVET Colleges (craft certificates to Higher National Diplomas)
  • Industry apprenticeships

TVET programmes are regulated by TVET Authority (TVETA) under the Ministry of Education.

📌 Source: TVET Authority Kenya; MoE TVET Policy

Kenya's CBC school calendar maintains the traditional three-term academic year:

  • Term 1: January–April (approximately 13 weeks)
  • Term 2: May–August (approximately 13 weeks)
  • Term 3: September–November (approximately 11–12 weeks)

The MoE publishes an official school calendar each year setting exact opening and closing dates. Public holidays, national events, and examination periods are factored in. Schools are expected to plan their schemes of work and assessment schedule in line with the official calendar.

📌 Source: MoE Annual School Calendar Circulars

CBC places strong emphasis on inclusive education. The BECF requires that curriculum designs, instructional materials, and assessments be adapted to meet the needs of learners with physical, sensory, intellectual, and learning disabilities.

Key provisions include:

  • Braille and large-print versions of learning materials for visually impaired learners
  • Kenya Sign Language as an alternative to Kiswahili for learners with hearing impairments
  • Adapted assessment tools and extended time allowances for learners with specific learning needs
  • Support for learners in special schools, integrated schools, and resource rooms
  • Training for teachers in differentiated instruction to accommodate diverse learners in the same classroom
📌 Source: KICD BECF, 2017; Special Needs Education Policy, Kenya

Senior Secondary School Pathways

10 Q&As

From Grade 10, all learners select one of three pathways:

  • STEM Pathway (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics): Compulsory subjects include Core Mathematics, English, Kiswahili, and Physical & Health Education. Learners then choose from Science subjects (Biology, Chemistry, Physics) and Applied STEM subjects (Computer Science, Agriculture, Home Science as applied science).
  • Social Sciences Pathway: Compulsory subjects include English, Kiswahili, and PHE. Electives drawn from: History & Citizenship, Geography, Business Studies, Economics, Religious Education, and Foreign Languages.
  • Arts and Sports Science Pathway: Compulsory subjects include English, Kiswahili, and PHE. Electives drawn from: Performing Arts (Music, Drama, Dance), Visual Arts and Design, Film and Media Production, and Sports Science.
📌 Source: KICD SSS Curriculum Designs, 2024–2025; MoE Pathway Policy

Pathway placement at Grade 10 is informed by multiple factors — not solely KJSEA scores:

  • KJSEA results: Performance in relevant subject areas is considered. Strong Mathematics and Science scores support STEM pathway placement.
  • School-Based Assessment (SBA) records: The continuous assessment record from Grades 7–9 is reviewed.
  • Aptitude and interest inventories: Schools administer guidance tools to assess learner strengths and interests.
  • Learner and parent choice: Learners and guardians express pathway preferences as part of the placement process.
  • School capacity: Available teachers, laboratories, and facilities in each pathway stream.

Schools are supported by Career Guidance and Counselling (CGC) teachers, who must have formal CGC training and TSC recognition.

No single exam score alone determines pathway placement. The process is holistic and guided, not purely meritocratic.
📌 Source: MoE Pathway Placement Guidelines, 2024

Pathway transfers are possible, particularly during the first term of Grade 10, subject to:

  • School principal approval
  • Review by the school's Guidance and Counselling teacher
  • Parent/guardian consent
  • Availability of space and resources in the desired pathway at that school

After the first term, transfers become more complicated because the learner will have missed foundational pathway content. Mid-year or inter-year transfers are handled on a case-by-case basis. Learners who transfer later may need additional support to catch up.

📌 Source: MoE Pathway Placement and Transfer Guidelines

In the STEM pathway, the subject structure is:

  • Compulsory for all STEM learners: Core Mathematics, English, Kiswahili, Physical and Health Education (PHE).
  • Pure Sciences (choose at least 2): Biology, Chemistry, Physics.
  • Applied STEM (choose at least 1): Computer Science, Agriculture, Home Science (applied science option).

Common STEM combinations used in practice:

  • Engineering track: Mathematics + Physics + Chemistry (+ Computer Science or Agriculture)
  • Medical track: Mathematics + Biology + Chemistry (+ Physics)
  • CS/Technology track: Mathematics + Computer Science + Physics or Chemistry
📌 Source: KICD STEM Pathway Curriculum Designs, Grade 10–12, 2024–2025

Compulsory for all Social Sciences learners: English, Kiswahili, Physical and Health Education.

Elective subjects within the Social Sciences cluster:

  • History and Citizenship Education
  • Geography
  • Business Studies
  • Economics
  • Christian Religious Education (CRE) / Islamic Religious Education (IRE) / Hindu Religious Education (HRE)
  • Foreign Language (French, German, Arabic, Mandarin — where offered)
  • Business and Entrepreneurship

Learners typically study 3–4 electives in addition to the compulsory subjects.

📌 Source: KICD Social Sciences Pathway Curriculum Designs, 2024–2025

Compulsory for all Arts & Sports Science learners: English, Kiswahili, Physical and Health Education.

Elective subjects within the Arts & Sports cluster:

  • Music
  • Dance
  • Drama / Performing Arts
  • Fine Art and Visual Design
  • Craft and Technology
  • Film and Media Production
  • Sports Science

This pathway leads to careers in professional performance, design, media, coaching, sports management, film production, and arts education.

📌 Source: KICD Arts & Sports Science Pathway Curriculum Designs, 2024–2025

Each pathway leads to distinct but equally valued outcomes:

  • STEM → engineering, medicine, technology, science research
  • Social Sciences → law, economics, governance, education, journalism
  • Arts & Sports → professional performance, design, media, sports

The MoE, KICD, and TSC all reinforce this equity principle in official communications. Schools are expected to counsel learners and parents accordingly.

📌 Source: KICD BECF, 2017; MoE Pathway Policy Statement

Core Mathematics is compulsory in the STEM pathway. In the Social Sciences and Arts & Sports Science pathways, learners take a General Mathematics or Applied Mathematics module rather than the full Core Mathematics programme — ensuring numeracy and quantitative reasoning skills are maintained across all pathways.

The specific mathematics requirement per pathway is still being finalised in some curriculum design documentation as of 2025, and schools should confirm the current requirement with their respective Sub-County TSC offices and KICD regional centres.

📌 Source: KICD Mathematics Curriculum Designs; MoE Communications 2024–2025

Each school is required to have a qualified Career Guidance and Counselling (CGC) teacher who facilitates pathway choice. This teacher:

  • Administers aptitude and interest inventories to Grade 8 and 9 learners
  • Organises pathway exposure sessions, career talks, and pathway expos
  • Counsels individual learners and their parents on pathway options
  • Reviews KJSEA results and SBA records to give evidence-based advice
  • Documents all guidance interactions in the school's records

Parents and guardians are integral to this process — the final pathway selection is made collaboratively between the learner, parents, and the school's guidance team.

📌 Source: TSC CGC Guidelines; MoE Pathway Placement Circulars

  • Most urban and well-resourced schools are able to offer all three pathways or will do so by 2026.
  • Smaller rural schools may initially offer only one or two pathways, depending on teacher availability and infrastructure.
  • The government has committed to expanding STEM facilities (labs, computers) to more schools and recruiting specialist teachers for Arts and Sports Science pathways.
  • Learners in schools not offering their desired pathway may be transferred to nearby schools that do — subject to MoE boarding and travel support policies.
📌 Source: MoE CBC Implementation Status Reports, 2023–2025

Assessment in CBC

10 Q&As

CBC uses a dual-component assessment model:

  • School-Based Assessment (SBA) — approximately 60%: This is the continuous assessment component, accumulated throughout the term and year. It includes Continuous Assessment Tests (CATs), projects, portfolios, Community Service Learning records, and teacher observations. The precise SBA weighting per subject is defined in individual KICD curriculum designs.
  • National Examination — approximately 40%: External assessments administered by KNEC at transition points (KPSEA, KJSEA, KCSE). For the final KCSE at Grade 12, this will be the main summative, standardised component.
Note: The exact SBA-to-examination ratio for KCSE is still being finalised by KNEC and MoE as of 2025. These percentages reflect the general CBC philosophy and interim guidelines — confirm with your school and KICD regional office for the most current ratios.
📌 Source: KICD Assessment Policy; KNEC CBC Assessment Framework

KICD prescribes 13 assessment tools for use across all subjects and grade levels. Teachers select the most appropriate tool(s) based on the learning outcome, strand, and activity:

  1. Observation Schedule — Records specific learner behaviours during an activity in structured format.
  2. Oral Questions and Answers — Verbal assessment of understanding through direct questioning.
  3. Written Tests — Pen-and-paper assessments for cognitive outcomes.
  4. Checklist — A binary (yes/no or done/not done) record of whether criteria are met.
  5. Rating Scale — A scale (e.g. 1–4, or Excellent/Good/Fair/Poor) for grading degree of performance.
  6. Rubric — Detailed scoring guide with descriptors for each level of performance on each criterion.
  7. Portfolio — A curated, reflective collection of a learner's work showing growth over time.
  8. Project — An extended task requiring application of knowledge to produce a tangible output.
  9. Anecdotal Records — Brief factual observations written by the teacher about a learner at a significant moment.
  10. Peer Assessment — Learners evaluate each other's work using agreed criteria.
  11. Self-Assessment — Learners reflect on and evaluate their own learning and performance.
  12. Questionnaire — Structured written questions gathering learner attitudes, knowledge, or perceptions.
  13. Interview — A directed verbal exchange to assess depth and breadth of understanding.
📌 Source: KICD BECF, 2017; KICD Teacher Guides; CBC Curriculum Designs

A rubric is a scoring guide that describes the expected quality of performance at each level for a given task or criterion. In CBC, rubrics are the most detailed and commonly required assessment tool for project work, presentations, and creative tasks.

There are two types used in CBC:

  • Analytic rubric: Breaks the task into separate criteria (e.g. Accuracy, Communication, Organisation, Creativity) and scores each independently. Gives learners detailed feedback on specific areas.
  • Holistic rubric: Gives a single overall score for the complete performance. Faster to use but less diagnostically specific.

CBC performance levels are typically described as: Exceeding Expectations (EE) | Meeting Expectations (ME) | Approaching Expectations (AE) | Below Expectations (BE).

📌 Source: KICD Assessment Policy; KICD Teacher Competency Framework

The exact number of CATs per term is specified in individual KICD curriculum designs for each subject and level. The typical structure for most subjects at Senior Secondary level is 2–3 CATs per term, administered at roughly equal intervals (e.g. after every 4–5 weeks of instruction).

CATs typically cover the content taught since the previous CAT or the beginning of term. Where three CATs are administered, KICD often recommends counting the best two out of three scores toward the final SBA tally.

In addition to CATs, the SBA component includes: project work, portfolios, community service learning documentation, and practicals (for science and practical subjects).

📌 Source: KICD Subject-Specific Curriculum Designs; KICD Assessment Guidelines

A portfolio is a purposeful, organised collection of a learner's work over time. Unlike a folder of all work produced, a CBC portfolio is selected evidence chosen by the learner (with teacher guidance) to demonstrate growth, achievement, and competency development.

A well-organised CBC portfolio typically contains:

  • Table of contents and a learner introduction ("Who I am as a learner")
  • Best-work samples from different strands and topics, each annotated
  • Draft and final versions of at least one piece of work (showing improvement)
  • One major project report or write-up
  • Community Service Learning records and reflections
  • Self-reflection entries (at least one per term)
  • Teacher feedback notes and marked assessment records

Portfolios are assessed using a rubric or checklist and contribute to the SBA grade.

📌 Source: KICD Assessment Policy; KICD Portfolio Guidelines

CBC uses a four-level performance scale for reporting learner achievement, particularly in lower and junior secondary levels:

  • EE — Exceeding Expectations: The learner demonstrates performance significantly above the expected level for their grade.
  • ME — Meeting Expectations: The learner demonstrates the expected level of performance for their grade consistently.
  • AE — Approaching Expectations: The learner shows progress toward the expected level but has not fully met it.
  • BE — Below Expectations: The learner is not yet meeting the expected level and requires additional support.

For Senior Secondary School and the KCSE examination, numerical marks and letter grade scales aligned to KNEC's grading system are used, as in 8-4-4 (A, A-, B+, B, B-, C+, C, C-, D+, D, D-, E).

📌 Source: KICD Assessment Reporting Guidelines; KNEC Grading Framework

Formative assessment happens during learning. Its purpose is to monitor progress, provide feedback, and help the teacher adjust instruction. Examples: class observations, oral Q&A, exit tickets, peer feedback, quick checklists. Formative assessment does not always produce a recorded grade — it guides the teaching process.

Summative assessment happens at the end of a unit, term, or learning cycle. Its purpose is to measure the overall achievement of learning outcomes. Examples: end-of-term CAT, KJSEA, KCSE, end-of-unit project. Summative results are recorded and contribute to the official SBA or examination grade.

CBC emphasises that both are equally important and that assessment should always serve learning — not just measure it.

📌 Source: KICD BECF, 2017; KICD Assessment Policy

SBA records are the responsibility of the class teacher for each subject. They are maintained in:

  • Individual assessment record books (one per subject per class)
  • Learner portfolios (kept by learners, reviewed and stamped by the teacher)
  • The school's digital or physical SBA register

SBA records are subject to verification by:

  • The school principal — internal quality assurance
  • TSC school inspectors — during scheduled inspection visits
  • KNEC moderators — who may visit schools as part of the KCSE moderation process
  • County/Sub-County education officers — as part of the national monitoring system

Falsification of SBA records is a disciplinary offence under the TSC Code of Conduct and Regulations for Teachers.

📌 Source: KICD SBA Guidelines; TSC Code of Conduct, 2015

Peer and self-assessment are powerful tools in CBC but require careful implementation to be educationally valid:

For peer assessment:

  • Learners must be given a clear rubric or checklist before they assess a peer's work.
  • Training sessions should precede the first formal peer assessment activity.
  • Feedback should be written, specific, and constructive — not just a score.
  • The teacher reviews all peer assessments before they are recorded.

For self-assessment:

  • Learners reflect on their own work against defined criteria.
  • Self-assessment forms part of the portfolio and must include written reflection.
  • It should not be used as the sole grade for a component — it is evidence of metacognition, not just a mark.
📌 Source: KICD Teacher Professional Development Guides

The full structure of KCSE for the first CBC cohort (expected to sit approximately 2028/2029) is still being finalised by KNEC and MoE as of 2025. What is known:

  • KCSE will remain the terminal national assessment at end of Grade 12.
  • It will assess subject knowledge alongside competencies developed through the chosen pathway.
  • The SBA component accumulated across Grades 10–12 will contribute to the final KCSE grade.
  • KNEC will define the mark scheme, subject combinations required for different university courses, and the minimum grade requirements.
  • The KCSE grade will be reported using letter grades (A to E), aligned with existing university admission requirements.

Teachers and learners are advised to monitor communications from KNEC and MoE as specific details are progressively released.

📌 Source: KNEC CBC Assessment Framework (ongoing updates); MoE Circulars

Learning Outcomes & Lesson Planning

8 Q&As

A Specific Learning Outcome (SLO) is a clear, measurable statement of what a learner should be able to do by the end of a lesson, unit, or term. SLOs are learner-centred and action-oriented — they describe observable behaviours, not teacher activities.

An SLO must be:

  • Learner-centred: Written from the learner's perspective ("The learner will be able to…") not the teacher's ("I will teach…")
  • Observable: Expressed using a verb that describes a visible, measurable action
  • Achievable: Realistic within the time, resources, and learner readiness available
  • Aligned: Directly linked to the KICD curriculum design strand and sub-strand
📌 Source: KICD BECF; KICD Curriculum Designs; KICD Teacher Guides

KICD recognises four models for writing Specific Learning Outcomes:

  • VOC Verb + Object + Context: The simplest and most widely used model for individual lesson SLOs. E.g. "Simplify [V] surds [O] using the product rule [C]"
  • ABCD Audience + Behaviour + Condition + Degree: Used for unit and scheme-level outcomes where the standard of performance needs to be specified. E.g. "Grade 10 learners (A) will solve quadratic equations (B) using the formula (C) with 80% accuracy (D)"
  • SMART Specific + Measurable + Achievable + Relevant + Time-bound: Used for project goals, term targets, and portfolio objectives.
  • MAGER Performance + Condition + Criterion: Used for practical tasks, laboratory work, and observable skill demonstrations. E.g. "Given a burette and sodium hydroxide solution (Condition), perform a titration (Performance) to within ±0.02 mL (Criterion)"
📌 Source: KICD Teacher Professional Development Materials; KICD Curriculum Designs

Bloom's Revised Taxonomy provides the framework for selecting the action verb in an SLO. The six levels, from lower-order to higher-order thinking, are:

  1. Remembering: Define, List, Name, Recall, State, Identify
  2. Understanding: Explain, Describe, Summarise, Classify, Compare
  3. Applying: Solve, Calculate, Demonstrate, Use, Apply, Perform
  4. Analysing: Analyse, Distinguish, Examine, Investigate, Categorise
  5. Evaluating: Evaluate, Justify, Critique, Assess, Defend, Recommend
  6. Creating: Design, Create, Develop, Compose, Invent, Build
CBC requires that SLOs progress through all levels across a term or unit — not just recalling facts, but also applying, analysing, and creating. Junior secondary learners should regularly encounter Levels 3–5 and senior secondary learners Levels 4–6.
📌 Source: KICD Teacher Professional Development Materials; KICD Curriculum Designs

KICD organises each subject curriculum in a three-tier hierarchy:

  • Strand: The broadest thematic area within a subject. E.g. in Grade 10 Mathematics: "Strand 1.0 — Numbers"
  • Sub-Strand: A more specific content area within a Strand. E.g. "Sub-Strand 1.1 — Surds and Indices"
  • Specific Learning Outcomes (SLOs): The individual, lesson-level competency statements within each Sub-Strand. E.g. "Simplify surds using the product rule in real-world contexts"

Teachers write their schemes of work and lesson plans by selecting the relevant Strand → Sub-Strand → SLOs from the KICD curriculum design for their subject and grade, then designing learning experiences and assessment tools to match.

📌 Source: KICD Subject Curriculum Designs (all grades and subjects)

Scheme of Work: A term-length document showing how the teacher plans to cover all SLOs in the curriculum design across all the lessons of the term. It is a planning grid that maps out, week by week and lesson by lesson: the Strand, Sub-Strand, specific SLO, learning experiences, resources, and assessment tools to be used. It must be prepared before the term begins and approved by the Head of Department.

Lesson Plan: A detailed, single-lesson document (covering one or two lessons) that expands on one row of the scheme of work. It contains: lesson SLOs (written using a prescribed model), prerequisite knowledge, resources, a full lesson development (Introduction / Main lesson / Consolidation / Closure), differentiation strategies, and post-lesson teacher reflection.

Both documents are required by the TSC and may be inspected during school visits. Failure to maintain current schemes of work and lesson plans is a performance issue under TSC's TPAD system.
📌 Source: KICD Teacher Guides; TSC TPAD Guidelines

Differentiated instruction (DI) is a teaching approach that adjusts the content, process, product, or learning environment to meet the diverse readiness levels, interests, and learning profiles of all learners in a class. It is not a single strategy but a mindset that guides lesson planning.

In CBC, DI is a professional requirement — not optional. The BECF and KICD teacher guides explicitly require teachers to plan for:

  • Support activities for learners below the expected level (scaffolded tasks, visual aids, reduced complexity)
  • Core activities for learners meeting expectations (standard lesson)
  • Extension activities for learners exceeding expectations (deeper application, independent research, additional challenge)

All three groups must be provided for in the lesson plan's differentiation section.

📌 Source: KICD BECF, 2017; KICD Teacher Guides; TSC Competency Framework

These are the most frequently observed errors during KICD training and TSC appraisal visits:

  • "The teacher will explain/teach..." — SLOs must be about the learner, not the teacher.
  • Using unobservable verbs — "Understand", "know", "appreciate", and "feel" cannot be directly observed or measured. Replace with: explain, demonstrate, solve, identify, analyse.
  • Writing too broad an outcome — "Solve mathematics problems" is not a measurable lesson SLO. Specify the type of problem and method: "Solve quadratic equations using the factorisation method".
  • Copying outcomes verbatim from the curriculum design — The curriculum design provides the broad expected outcomes; lesson SLOs must be narrower and specific to what will be taught in that lesson.
  • Writing outcomes that cannot be assessed in the lesson — If you write the outcome, you must also plan how you will assess it in that same lesson.
📌 Source: KICD CBC Teacher Retooling Programme Materials

In the 8-4-4 system, syllabi were organised by topics — discrete, sequential content units. Each topic was taught, examined, and largely set aside. In CBC, curriculum is organised by strands and sub-strands:

  • Strands are thematic, broad learning areas that recur and deepen across multiple grades. E.g. the "Data Handling and Probability" strand in Mathematics appears in Grade 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, and 12 — each year building on the prior year's competencies.
  • Sub-strands are the specific content areas within each strand, progressively developed over the years.

This spiral curriculum approach ensures that foundational competencies are reinforced and deepened rather than taught once and abandoned. A learner does not simply "finish" statistics in Grade 8 — they return to it with greater sophistication in Grade 10 and 11.

📌 Source: KICD BECF, 2017; KICD Curriculum Designs

Teachers, TSC & Professional Standards

10 Q&As

To be registered and practice as a teacher in Kenya under CBC, a person must:

  • Hold a minimum of a Bachelor of Education (BEd) degree from a recognised university, with a relevant subject specialisation (for primary and secondary school) OR a Bachelor's degree in a relevant subject plus a Post-Graduate Diploma in Education (PGDE).
  • Pass the TSC Competency Assessment — a professional certification process assessing teaching knowledge and skills.
  • Hold a valid Certificate of Good Conduct from the Kenya Police / Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI).
  • Have completed CBC retooling training as facilitated by KICD or TSC-accredited providers.
  • Not appear on the National Teacher Registration exclusion list (for those deregistered for professional misconduct).
📌 Source: TSC Act, 2012; TSC Registration Guidelines; TSC Circular on CBC Retooling

CBC retooling is a structured professional development programme designed to equip existing teachers — particularly those trained under the 8-4-4 system — with the knowledge, skills, and dispositions required to deliver CBC effectively.

Retooling covers:

  • CBC philosophy, core competencies, and the BECF
  • Competency-based pedagogy and learner-centred instruction
  • Formative assessment and use of the 13 KICD assessment tools
  • Writing learning outcomes using the four prescribed models
  • Differentiated instruction for diverse learners
  • ICT integration in teaching and learning
  • Strand-based curriculum organisation and lesson/scheme planning

Retooling is mandatory for all practising teachers as a condition of continued TSC registration renewal and TPAD appraisal. Both face-to-face and online (KICD e-learning platform) retooling options exist.

📌 Source: TSC Circular on CBC Retooling; KICD CPD Programme

TPAD — the Teacher Performance Appraisal and Development System — is TSC's framework for evaluating the professional performance of all registered teachers. Under CBC, TPAD assessments focus on:

  • Quality of scheme of work and lesson plan preparation (including correct SLOs and differentiation)
  • Use of a range of assessment tools (not just written tests)
  • Evidence of learner progress in SBA records
  • Maintenance of portfolios and project documentation
  • Community engagement facilitation (CSL support)
  • Professional development and retooling completion
  • Classroom management and creation of a conducive learning environment

TPAD scores are used by TSC for promotion decisions, deployment, and — in cases of persistent poor performance — remediation or disciplinary action.

📌 Source: TSC TPAD Guidelines, 2016 (updated 2022); TSC Act, 2012

All TSC-registered teachers are required to participate in Continuing Professional Development (CPD) to maintain their registration and TPAD standing. The TSC mandates a minimum of 30 CPD hours per year, which can be earned through:

  • KICD-facilitated CBC retooling modules (face-to-face or online)
  • TSC-accredited subject-specific workshops and seminars
  • School-based professional learning communities (PLCs)
  • Relevant university courses or diplomas
  • Published research and peer-reviewed contributions to education
  • Participation in curriculum development activities with KICD

CPD records must be documented and submitted as part of TPAD appraisal.

📌 Source: TSC CPD Policy; TSC TPAD Framework

The TSC Code of Regulations for Teachers (2015) governs the professional conduct of all registered teachers. Key conduct expectations include:

  • Delivering quality, learner-centred instruction consistent with CBC guidelines
  • Maintaining accurate, honest assessment records — including SBA
  • Protecting the rights, dignity, and safety of all learners at all times
  • Not engaging in corporal punishment (prohibited under Kenyan law)
  • Maintaining professional boundaries with learners
  • Reporting cases of child abuse or neglect through the appropriate channels
  • Not using school resources, learner data, or assessment records for personal gain
  • Upholding examination integrity — any involvement in exam malpractice is a criminal and professional offence

Violations are investigated by the TSC Disciplinary Committee, which has the authority to reprimand, suspend, or deregister a teacher.

📌 Source: TSC Code of Regulations for Teachers, 2015; TSC Act, 2012

For teaching in the STEM pathway at Senior Secondary level:

  • Mathematics: A Bachelor of Education (Mathematics) or Bachelor of Science (Mathematics) + PGDE. The teacher must demonstrate strong content knowledge in Pure Mathematics including calculus, statistics, algebra, and geometry to Grade 12 level.
  • Computer Science: A Bachelor of Education (Computer Science) or Bachelor of Science/Computer Science + PGDE. Additionally, TSC and KICD expect Computer Science teachers to demonstrate practical programming competence — including proficiency in at least one high-level language (Python is recommended by KICD for Grade 10–11) and database management concepts.
  • Physics, Chemistry, Biology: Respective BEd specialisation or BSc + PGDE, with valid lab safety certification.

Teachers who qualified in 8-4-4 subject areas that did not previously include Computer Science (e.g., Mathematics + Business Studies) may need to undertake additional subject upgrading to qualify for CS teaching.

📌 Source: TSC Subject Deployment Policy; KICD CS Curriculum Design, 2024–2025

As of 2024/2025, the TSC and KICD report that:

  • Over 246,000 teachers have participated in at least one CBC retooling module.
  • The TSC has a registered teacher base of approximately 350,000 across public primary and secondary schools.
  • Training for Senior Secondary School (Grades 10–12) pathway teachers is ongoing, with KICD prioritising pathway-specific curriculum familiarisation for Grade 10 teachers in 2025.
  • A significant gap exists in teachers qualified for Computer Science, Creative Arts, and Sports Science — subjects that were limited or absent in 8-4-4.

The government is working to address the teacher gap through TSC recruitment drives and university curriculum adjustments to produce more CBC-ready graduates.

📌 Source: TSC Annual Report 2023/2024; KICD CBC Status Reports

The school principal (or head teacher) plays a critical leadership and oversight role in CBC implementation. Their key responsibilities include:

  • Ensuring all teachers have prepared and are using current, KICD-aligned schemes of work and lesson plans
  • Approving timetables that provide sufficient time for practical, project, and CSL activities — not just academic lessons
  • Overseeing the school's SBA records, portfolio system, and assessment documentation
  • Ensuring teachers complete required CBC retooling and CPD hours
  • Facilitating pathway selection processes with the CGC teacher at the Grade 9–10 transition
  • Maintaining the school's KICD-approved instructional materials and laboratory/equipment inventory
  • Liaising with Sub-County Education Officers on CBC implementation issues
📌 Source: MoE School Management Guidelines; TSC Institutional Leadership Standards

Managing the CBC assessment workload in classes of 40+ learners is a real challenge. KICD and TSC advise teachers to:

  • Stagger assessments: Not all tools need to be used in every lesson. Plan which tools are used when, across the whole term.
  • Use group-based tools: Observation schedules and projects can be applied to groups of 5–8, reducing individual assessment frequency.
  • Leverage peer and self-assessment: These tools actively involve learners in the process and reduce the teacher's individual marking load while building metacognition.
  • Pre-prepare record templates: Checklists and rating scales with pre-printed learner names significantly speed up real-time recording.
  • Maintain a rolling portfolio review schedule: Review 8–10 portfolios per week rather than all at once at term end.
📌 Source: KICD CBC Teacher Professional Development Guides

Under TSC and MoE guidelines, the designated CGC teacher in a secondary school should ideally hold:

  • A Bachelor of Education (Counselling) or a BEd with a post-graduate qualification in Counselling or Career Guidance
  • TSC registration with CGC as a recognised specialty area
  • Training in standardised aptitude and interest assessment instruments

In practice, many schools do not yet have a formally qualified CGC specialist, and the role is fulfilled by a teacher with relevant experience plus MoE/TSC short-course CGC training. The MoE is working to standardise CGC teacher deployment alongside the Grade 9–10 pathway transition.

📌 Source: MoE CGC Policy; TSC Deployment Guidelines

Parents & Guardians

8 Q&As

CBC is designed on the principle that education happens in the home and community as much as in the classroom. Parents are active partners in CBC implementation. Specific parental roles include:

  • Supporting Community Service Learning (CSL) activities — supervising and providing sign-off for CSL assignments done at home or in the community
  • Providing a supportive home environment for project work — including materials, workspace, and access to relevant community contacts
  • Attending scheduled parent-teacher conferences to review their child's SBA progress and portfolio
  • Participating in pathway guidance discussions at Grade 9 — providing input on family context, career aspirations, and the child's interests
  • Not placing excessive pressure on children to pursue a particular pathway against the child's aptitude and interests
📌 Source: KICD BECF, 2017; MoE Parent Engagement Guidelines

CBC progress reports for lower and junior secondary levels use the four-level performance scale rather than percentage marks:

  • EE — Exceeding Expectations: Your child is performing above the expected level. This is excellent — they may benefit from additional challenge or extension activities.
  • ME — Meeting Expectations: Your child is performing at the expected level for their grade. This is the target level and means they are progressing well.
  • AE — Approaching Expectations: Your child is making progress but has not yet consistently met the expected level. Additional support and practice at home would be beneficial.
  • BE — Below Expectations: Your child requires significant additional support. You should meet with their teacher to discuss a support plan and home-based reinforcement strategies.

For Senior Secondary School, reports will use numerical marks and letter grades (A to E) aligned with KCSE.

For public schools in Kenya:

  • Primary School (Grades 1–6): The government has provided KICD-approved textbooks and workbooks through a phased procurement programme. In many schools, core textbooks are available free of charge through the school.
  • Junior Secondary School (Grades 7–9): Government procurement of KICD-approved JSS textbooks has been ongoing from 2023. Access varies by school and county. Parents may be asked to supplement with specific reference books.
  • Senior Secondary School (Grades 10–12): Textbook procurement for the pioneer Grade 10 cohort (2025) is in progress. The situation will become clearer as the cohort progresses.

Project and portfolio materials (stationery, files, printing) are generally the responsibility of parents, within the bounds of each school's reasonable policy.

📌 Source: MoE Textbook Procurement Reports; Kenya Basic Education Act, 2013

Some learners and families have reported feeling overwhelmed, particularly in the early stages of adaptation to CBC's project and portfolio requirements. Here is important context:

  • CBC is designed to reduce the single-exam pressure of 8-4-4, not replace one form of stress with another. If your child is regularly overwhelmed, this may signal implementation issues at the school level rather than a design flaw.
  • Projects should be manageable within school time, with some — but not unreasonable — home-based component. If a project is consuming several hours daily, speak with the teacher.
  • The KICD curriculum designs specify how long each project should take. Teachers are not supposed to assign multiple major projects simultaneously.

If you have specific concerns, you have the right to raise them with your child's class teacher, the Head of Department, or the school principal. Parents can also raise systemic concerns through School Management Committees (SMCs) or Parent-Teacher Associations (PTAs).

  • Board of Governors (BoG): For secondary schools. Parent representatives on the BoG participate in school policy decisions, including curriculum implementation oversight.

  • County Education Forums: County education offices periodically hold public forums on CBC implementation where parents can contribute.

  • AgriTech and HealthTech: Technology applied to agriculture and healthcare is among the fastest-growing sectors in Kenya and East Africa.

One of the explicit design goals of CBC, as stated in the BECF, is to produce graduates who are competitive both locally and globally. The curriculum achieves this through:

  • 21st-century competencies: The 7 core competencies — especially Digital Literacy, Critical Thinking, Creativity, and Collaboration — are the same competencies global employers and universities seek.
  • Project-based learning: Builds portfolio-quality work product and demonstrates applied skills — something global universities and employers increasingly look for beyond exam certificates.
  • Pathway specialisation: Deeper subject knowledge from Grade 10 produces graduates with more focused, market-relevant expertise.
  • Digital literacy integration: CBC requires ICT to be integrated across all subjects — not taught as a standalone once-weekly lesson.
  • Community Service Learning: Develops the civic awareness and professional soft skills increasingly required in global workplaces.
📌 Source: KICD BECF, 2017; Kenya Vision 2030 Education Pillar

Preparations are underway but uneven. As of 2025:

  • The Kenya Universities and Colleges Central Placement Service (KUCCPS) is working with MoE and KNEC to align admission requirements with CBC subject combinations.
  • Some universities have begun reviewing their first-year course structures to account for the changed prior learning profile of CBC graduates — particularly in STEM programmes that will now include Computer Science as a potential prerequisite.
  • The Commission for University Education (CUE) is developing guidelines for how CBC-stream applicants' qualifications will be evaluated.
  • There is an acknowledged transition challenge: the last 8-4-4 KCSE cohort and the first CBC KCSE cohort will be seeking university places in the same period — requiring careful management.

Students and parents are advised to monitor KUCCPS, CUE, and MoE communications closely from 2027 onwards.

📌 Source: CUE; KUCCPS; MoE Higher Education Transition Planning

Yes — and the CBC framework explicitly works to change the historical stigma around TVET in Kenya. The BECF positions TVET as an equally valid and dignified path to skilled employment and economic contribution:

  • Kenya has a significant and growing skills gap in technical trades — electricians, plumbers, welders, mechanics, ICT technicians — that TVET graduates are uniquely positioned to fill.
  • TVET graduates in specialised fields can earn competitive and often better incomes than many degree-holding graduates, particularly in the first 5–10 years of their careers.
  • TVET programmes lead to recognised qualifications (Craft Certificate, Diploma, Higher National Diploma) regulated by TVETA and the Kenya National Qualifications Authority (KNQA).
  • Advanced TVET programmes can articulate into university degrees — a learner with a TVET Diploma may enter a university degree programme at advanced standing in some universities.
📌 Source: TVET Authority (TVETA); Kenya National Qualifications Framework; KICD BECF
